# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Oh My Bug ?! serves software engineers who triage, diagnose, implement, and validate bug fixes and feature changes in local repositories. They use it as a focused desktop console while reviewing external requests, agent assessments, implementation progress, and visual acceptance evidence. Their primary job is to reach a trustworthy decision quickly without surrendering control of consequential actions.

## Product Purpose

Oh My Bug ?! unifies Manual, Sentry, and DingTalk inputs into a local Issue workflow, coordinates Codex Agent analysis and implementation, and preserves explicit human gates before an assessment is accepted or a change is closed. Success means engineers can understand the rationale and implementation plan, inspect the evidence, grant narrowly scoped approval, and finish the workflow without losing context or exposing secrets.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. The product should feel like a dependable engineering instrument: dense enough for expert work, restrained enough to keep attention on evidence, and explicit whenever user authority or risk is involved.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a generic chatbot, analytics dashboard, marketing site, or decorative AI control center. Avoid copied Linear branding, oversized cards, ambient gradients, glass effects, gratuitous animation, ambiguous approvals, and interfaces that hide failures behind optimistic summaries.

## Design Principles

1. Earn attention: current decisions, failures, and pending approvals lead; navigation and completed background work recede.
2. Preserve human authority: explain what every approval unlocks and never make irreversible action accidental.
3. Make evidence inspectable: root cause, revisions, visual proof, commands, and activity remain traceable without becoming a chat transcript.
4. Favor expert flow: use predictable, keyboard-accessible patterns and compact information density without sacrificing clarity.
5. Keep state truthful: status language, color, iconography, and validation consistently reflect the real system state.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Support visible keyboard focus, logical focus order, accessible names for icon-only controls, non-color state cues, reduced motion, polite live announcements, and operation at 200% zoom. Dense desktop controls must expand to appropriate touch targets for coarse pointers.
